Survival

The objective of survival analysis it to understand the factors influencing mortality rates.

Most studies of survival use a “failure time”, a.k.a. “time to event” approach.

These are continuous-time models in which the random variable of interest is the survival time (\(T\)).

Censoring

Typically, we don’t observer survival time for all individuals because some are still alive at the end of the observation period, either because transmitter batteries die or we stop monitoring.

These survival times are “right censored”, which means that we know they occurred after the observation window, but we don’t know their exact value.

There are several types of censoring: - Right censoring (common): survival time is in the interval \((U,\infty]\) - Left censoring (rare): survival time is in the interval \([-\infty,L)\) - Interval censoring (rare): survival time is in \((a,b]\).

Survival

There are 4 quantities that you must understand:

  1. Hazard \(\lambda(t)\)
    • The instantaneous mortality risk
  2. Cummulative hazard \(\Lambda(t)\)
    • The cummulative mortality risk
  3. Survivorship \(S(t)\)
    • The probability of surviving until time \(t\)
  4. Probability density \(p(t)\)
    • The relative probability of dying at time \(t\)

Continuous-time models

Hazard

The continuous-time definition of the hazard rate is the instantaneous mortality risk, conditional on surviving to time \(t\): \[ \lambda(t) = \lim_{\Delta_t \to 0} \Pr(t \le T < t+\Delta_t | T\ge t)/\Delta_t \]

The cumulative hazard can be thought of as the sum of the risks up to time \(t\): \[ \Lambda(t) = \int_{0}^t \lambda(j) \,\mathrm{d}j \]

Survivorship and probability density

Survivorship is the probability of surviving to time \(t\) or longer: \[ % S(t) = \Pr(T \ge t) = \exp\left(-\int_{0}^t \lambda(j) \,dj\right) S(t) = \Pr(T \ge t) = \exp(-\Lambda(t)) \]

The probability density is given by the probability of surviving to, and then dying at, time \(t\) \[ p(t) = S(t)\lambda(t) %\exp\left(-\int_{0}^t \lambda(j) \,dj\right) \]

Exponential survival times

The simplest survival model is the “exponential model”, which assumes that the hazard is constant over time: \(\lambda = \lambda(t)\).

This implies: - \(S(t) = \exp(-\lambda t)\) - \(p(t) = \lambda \exp(-\lambda t)\)

The mean life expectancy is: \(\mu = E(T) = 1/\lambda\).

Kaplan-Meier

Before we fit a model, it can be useful to visualize a non-parametric estimator of the survivorship curve.

\[ \hat{S}(t) = \prod_{j:a_j < t} \left(1 - \frac{d_j}{r_j}\right) \]

where - \(a_j\) is the time interval - \(d_j\) is the number of mortalities in time interval \(j\) - \(r_j\) is the number of individuals “at risk” at the beginning of interval \(j\)

Bayesian inference

In JAGS, the recommended way of dealing with censoring is with the dinterval distribution.

surv-exp.jag

model{

beta0 ~ dnorm(0, 0.1)
beta1 ~ dnorm(0, 0.1)

for(i in 1:n) {
  lambda[i] <- exp(beta0 + beta1*x[i])
  # Survival times are only observed when censored=0 
  survivalTime[i] ~ dexp(lambda[i])
  censored[i] ~ dinterval(survivalTime[i], censorTime[i])
}

}

Discrete-time models

Discrete-time survival models

A simple way of modeling survival is to discretize time and use the following model: \[ z_{i,t} \sim \mathrm{Bern}(z_{i,t-1}\times \phi_t) \]

where \(z_{i,t}\) indicates if individual \(i\) is alive at time \(t\) and \(\phi\) is the probability of surviving the time interval.

In discrete time we have: - Hazard: \(\lambda_t = 1-\phi_t = \Pr(t \le T < t+1 | T\ge t)\) - Survivorship: \(S_t = \prod_{j=1}^t \phi_j\) - Probability density: \(p(t) = S_t \lambda_t\)

Simulating discrete-time survival data

Parameters and dimensions

maxTime <- 10           ## Time period
n <- 25                 ## nIndividuals
phi <- 0.7              ## Survival probability over 1 time step

Generate \(z\), the alive-dead indicator

z <- matrix(NA, n, maxTime)
first <- rpois(n, 1)+1  ## random release dates
for(i in 1:n) {
    z[i,first[i]] <- 1  ## Known alive at release
    for(t in (first[i]+1):maxTime) {
        z[i,t] <- rbinom(1, 1, z[i,t-1]*phi) ## Alive/dead state
    }
}

Competing risks

Competing risks models are used when there are multiple ways of dying, and we’re interested in the cause-specific mortality risk, represented by \(K\) hazard functions: \[ \lambda_{k,t} = \exp(\beta_{0,k} + \beta_{1,k}x_{t} + \cdots) \]

We can model the conditional probability of dying during interval \(t\), with a multinomial link function: \[ \pi_{k,t} = \frac{\lambda_{k,t}}{1+\sum_{j=1}^K \lambda_{j,t}} \] where the final multinomial cell probability is the probability of surviving: \(\pi_{K+1,t} = 1-\sum_{k=1}^K \pi_{k,t}\).

The data can be modeled with a categorical distribution: \[ z_{i,t} \sim \mathrm{Cat}({\bm \pi}_t | \mathrm{alive\; at\;} t-1) \]

Competing risks

Suppose you can die in 3 ways: killed by cougar, bear, or wolf

beta0 <- c(-6, -4, -3) ## log-hazard. No covariates
lambda <- exp(beta0)   ## Cause-specific hazard
pi <- lambda / (1+sum(lambda))
pi[4] <- 1-sum(pi)     ## Probability of surviving t to t+1

Set-up the data as a multi-state indicator

nDeer <- 100

Competing risks

surv-dtime-comp-risks.jag

model{

## z states could be (for example):
## 1=killed by cougar
## 2=killed by bear
## 3=killed by wolf
## 4=alive
for(k in 1:nRisks) {
  beta0[k] ~ dnorm(0, 0.1)          ## Intercept only model
  lambda[k] <- exp(beta0[k])        ## Cause-specific hazard
  pi[k] <- lambda[k]/(1+Lambda)     ## Pr(killed by cause k)
  }
Lambda <- sum(lambda)               ## Total risk
pi[nRisks+1] <- 1-sum(pi[1:nRisks]) ## Pr(not killed)
Phi[nRisks+1,] <- pi

for(i in 1:n) {
  for(t in (first[i]+1):maxTime) {
    z[i,t] ~ dcat(Phi[z[i,t-1],1:4])
  }
}

}

Assumptions of failure time models

Random sampling - Very hard to do in practice

Independent survival times - Problematic for gregarious species

Mortality times are known exactly - Also hard

Censoring is random and independent of survival - This will be a problem if transmitters fail when animals die, and you don’t know if they died.

Well-defined time origin - Animals don’t have to be released at the same time
